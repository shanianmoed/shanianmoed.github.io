About

My work sits where materials physics meets device engineering. I use density functional theory to understand the electronic structure of two-dimensional materials, then carry those insights into device-level models of transistors and solar cells built in COMSOL and TCAD.

I am especially interested in how strain, defects, stacking and interfaces change band structure and transport, and in what those changes mean for the next generation of low-power nanoelectronics and efficient photovoltaics.

Research areas

  • DFT of 2D materials

    First-principles study of monolayers and heterostructures: band structure, density of states, effective mass, and how strain, doping and defects tune them.

  • Nanoelectronics

    Quantum and ballistic transport in nanoscale devices, and how material choice sets the limits of scaling, switching and power.

  • CNTFETs

    Carbon nanotube field-effect transistors: chirality-dependent bandgap, contact effects, and performance against silicon at short channel lengths.

  • HEMTs

    High-electron-mobility transistors and the two-dimensional electron gas at heterointerfaces, for high-frequency and high-power electronics.

  • Photovoltaic cells

    Absorber and heterojunction design, band alignment and loss mechanisms, with simulated J–V behaviour and efficiency.

  • COMSOL & TCAD modeling

    Device and multiphysics simulation: drift–diffusion, Poisson–Schrödinger, thermal and optical coupling, from structure to characteristics.
